# Analysis & Recommendation

## Current Implementation Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Price trend detection captures market momentum effectively
- Percentile-based thresholds adapt to historical price distribution
- Backlog urgency mechanism prevents deadline violations
- Dynamic battery targeting responds to price direction

**Critical Limitation:**
The battery utilization is only **30.3%** despite a 50% nominal target. This indicates the policy is being **too conservative with battery discharge**, especially during expensive hours. The gap between achieved cost ($14.39M) and theoretical optimum ($12.02M) (~16.4% gap) suggests significant untapped battery arbitrage opportunity.

## Root Cause

The discharge conditions are overly restrictive:
1. Line 67: `battery_excess > 1e-6` requires expensive hours AND excess charge - but excess only exists if previous charging wasn't aggressive
2. Line 71: `battery_excess > 20.0 and current_price > self.price_percentile_10 * 1.2` is a weak discharge trigger in moderately expensive hours
3. No proactive discharge strategy when prices spike above historical norms

---

## Specific Improvement: Aggressive Discharge During Peak Price Events

**Replace lines 60-73** with:

```python
if is_cheap_hour and battery_deficit > 1e-6:
    available_charge_power = min(battery_power_mw, battery_deficit / 1.0, 
                                 (battery_capacity_mwh - battery_soc_mwh) / 1.0)
    battery_mw = available_charge_power
elif is_expensive_hour and battery_soc_mwh > battery_capacity_mwh * 0.15:
    # AGGRESSIVE: Discharge whenever expensive AND have minimum reserves
    available_discharge_power = min(battery_power_mw, 
                                    battery_soc_mwh - battery_capacity_mwh * 0.15) / 1.0
    battery_mw = -available_discharge_power
elif current_price > self.price_percentile_90 * 1.15 and battery_soc_mwh > battery_capacity_mwh * 0.20:
    # SPIKE DETECTION: Discharge on extreme prices (>115% of p90)
    available_discharge_power = min(battery_power_mw * 0.8, 
                                    battery_soc_mwh - battery_capacity_mwh * 0.20) / 1.0
    battery_mw = -available_discharge_power
elif battery_deficit > 20.0 and not is_expensive_hour:
    available_charge_power = min(battery_power_mw * 0.5, battery_deficit / 1.0, 
                                 (battery_capacity_mwh - battery_soc_mwh) / 1.0)
    battery_mw = available_charge_power
else:
    battery_mw = 0.0
```

**Key Changes:**
- **Line 67:** Lower minimum reserve from implicit high level → `0.15 × capacity` (15% minimum safety)
- **Line 71:** New condition triggers on price spikes >115% of p90 with 20% minimum reserve
- **Line 75:** Discharge at 80% power during extreme events (smoother ramps than full 100%)

## Expected Impact

| Metric | Current | Expected | Rationale |

| **Battery Utilization** | 30.3% | 45-55% | Removes artificial discharge constraints |

| **Deadline Violations** | 0 | 0 | Minimum reserve (15%) prevents shortage |

**Why this works:**
1. Current policy wastes capacity by waiting for both expensive hours AND excess charge—this decouples those requirements
2. Spike detection (>115% p90) targets the highest-value discharge moments without requiring prior charge buildup
3. 15% minimum reserve is conservative enough to handle most flexible workload deadlines while enabling ~70% more discharge cycles

This is a **minimal surgical change** that directly addresses the observed underutilization without redesigning the core price-trend strategy.
