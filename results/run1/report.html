APS Replication Report

Replicating "Adaptive Self-Improvement for Smarter Energy Systems using Agentic Policy Search" (Sommer, Bazan, Babaeian, Fellerer, Powell & German, FAU Erlangen-Nürnberg / Princeton) — LLM agents that write, evaluate, and iteratively refine executable battery-control policies for a residential energy system.

Verdict: the headline claim replicates; the convergence claim does not. Within 10 iterations, every one of 10 episodes produced a profitable battery policy, and the best (−6.08 €) lands within 0.24 € (96 % of the achievable gap) of the perfect-foresight optimum — matching the paper's "up to −6.20 €, near the −6.67 € optimum" result. However, the paper's declining median cost trajectory did not reproduce: with a weaker code model, the meta-level's explore/refine loop degrades the median after iteration 2, amplifying the instability the authors themselves report in later iterations.

What was replicated

The full three-level APS architecture (paper Sections 5–7, Algorithm 1):

  • Level 1 — Simulation. 7-day residential energy system at 60-min resolution, seed 42: 10 kWh battery (90 % round-trip efficiency, 50 % initial SoC, +10/−5 kW power limits), 5 kWp sinusoidal PV with irradiance noise, base + morning/evening-peak load, volatile buy price (mean 0.35 €/kWh, 10 % volatility), fixed 0.08 €/kWh feed-in tariff.
  • Level 2 — Policy generation. An LLM turns a task description into a Python Policy.take_action() class (paper's verbatim prompt and interface), post-processed (Φ) and executed in a sandboxed subprocess; runtime errors trigger the paper's repair prompt for up to 5 attempts, then the iteration restarts.
  • Level 3 — Meta-policy. A second LLM receives cost/utilization/SoC metrics and the current implementation, and emits the next task description under the paper's greedy rule: refine after improvement, explore otherwise, with automatic switch to exploration on stagnation.

Substitutions: Claude Haiku 4.5 (fresh stateless call per prompt) stands in for Gemini 2.5 Flash Preview; 10 episodes were run instead of 20 (compute budget), each with 10 iterations as in the paper.

Deterministic benchmarks calibrate closely

The paper's noise processes are under-specified, so free shape parameters (peak widths, PV irradiance factor, price-noise level) were calibrated on seed 42 against the paper's three published reference costs. The perfect-foresight linear program (Appendix D) then reproduces:

Why the median diverges here

Two mechanisms, both visible in the transcripts, compound with a small code model. First, strong simple policies appear immediately (iteration 1–2), so the greedy rule rarely registers an improvement, and the meta-level switches to exploration almost permanently. Second, each exploration instruction (6–9 kB of ambitious strategy text) pushes the generator toward increasingly baroque implementations whose unit errors — e.g. price thresholds of 3.5 €/kWh against actual prices of ~0.35 €/kWh — buy heavily from the grid. The paper's discussion anticipates exactly this failure class ("further adjustments tend to degrade quality" after saturation, myopic selection criterion, minimal environmental context in prompts); with Haiku 4.5 in place of Gemini 2.5 Flash, the effect dominates the median rather than the tail. Since the search keeps the best-so-far policy, the deployable result is unaffected.

Environment

Deviations and limitations

  • 10 episodes instead of 20; medians over 10 runs are noisier.
  • Different LLM (Haiku 4.5); the divergence finding is therefore a statement about APS-with-a-weaker-model, not a contradiction of the paper's exact setup.
  • Exogenous noise processes reconstructed by calibration against three published costs; exact series differ from the authors'.
  • Iteration restarts capped at 2 (paper: unbounded); the cap was never reached.
  • No statistical outliers were removed (the paper removed two runs); our distribution's heavy upper tail is structural rather than two-point.
